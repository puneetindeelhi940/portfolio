1 Model details

A senior design-leadership system trained continuously from 2006 to 2026 across four organisations and four problem domains. Optimised for the regime where the problem is not yet well-formed, the organisation is not yet aligned, and the technology is not yet stable — the regime most AI product work currently occupies.

2 Intended use

  • Standing up an AI design practice inside an organisation that does not yet have one.
  • Taking a GenAI surface from demonstration to production traffic, with legal and brand review intact.
  • Scaling and repairing multi-disciplinary design orgs across time zones.
  • Design in regulated or safety-critical contexts — aviation, healthcare, enterprise.
  • Translating design outcomes into the language of finance and the C-suite.
  • Brand identity from zero. Systems thinker before stylist. Hire a brand designer.
  • Seed-stage improvisation. Trained on large institutions; the reflexes are institutional.
  • Consumer-social products. No meaningful signal in the training data.
  • Environments that want design to decorate a settled decision. Predictable degradation.

4 Limitations

Known weaknesses

Institutional bias. Seventeen of twenty years inside two large companies. Excellent at moving slow organisations; less rehearsed at the weekly pivots of a small one.

Front-loads disagreement. Surfaces unfunded tradeoffs early. Some teams read this as friction; it is a deliberate setting, and it can be tuned but not removed.

Attribution uncertainty on one benchmark. The 40% figure is a real award with a real mechanism — specification decisions moved earlier, cutting counted change requests — but design attribution in a multi-cause system deserves a wider error bar than an award implies.

Failure mode to watch

Given an under-specified brief and no access to the business metric, this system will go and find the metric rather than proceed. In organisations where that access is genuinely withheld, expect stall rather than graceful degradation.

5 Ethical considerations

The AI work here is personalisation and intent estimation — systems that infer what a person wants and reshape themselves accordingly. That capability sits one design decision away from manipulation. The behavioural-science nudges in the Dell initiative were bounded deliberately: optimised for user delight and ROI, in that order, because a nudge that wins the quarter and loses the customer is a defect wearing a metric.

This is also the reason for the Philosophy M.A. — what may a system justifiably infer, and at what confidence does inference become presumption? — pursued during the AI work rather than before it.

6 Caveats and recommendations

Recommended prompt

Best results come from handing this system an ambiguous, politically contested problem with a real business metric attached, and letting it reframe the question before it designs anything. Worst results come from handing it a finished decision and asking for a skin.
