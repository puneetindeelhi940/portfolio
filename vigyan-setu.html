§ 00 CONTEXT // The day Bharat Mandapam buckled

Day 1: The System That Wasn't There

India AI Impact Summit, February 16, 2026. Bharat Mandapam, New Delhi. 60,000 people arrived at gates within 3 hours. The venue's maximum throughput was designed for 15,000/hour. Gates closed for approximately 6 hours. Temperature outside: 28°C, no shade infrastructure. 250,000 registered attendees across the week, 20+ heads of state, and no operating system to manage any of it.

The Promise

"The world's largest AI summit." Ministerial sessions on responsible AI, sovereign compute, and agentic systems. 300+ exhibitors. Networking with global leaders. A showcase for India's digital ambitions.

The Reality

Queue collapse at every gate. VVIP motorcades shutting access for hours. No indoor wayfinding. Wi-Fi failure on Day 1. Zero networking infrastructure beyond exchanging business cards. WhatsApp groups as the only communication channel.

Why This Is Bigger Than One Bad Week

India hosts approximately 40 national-scale events per year — G20 summits, Vibrant Gujarat, DefExpo, Aero India, Republic Day celebrations. None of them have an operating system. Each one reinvents (or fails to invent) crowd management, credentialing, and communications from scratch.

The Reframe

Vigyan Setu is not a ticketing app. It sits alongside Aarogya Setu, DigiLocker, UPI, and DigiYatra as national digital infrastructure. India solved payments (UPI), health credentialing (Aarogya Setu), airport identity (DigiYatra), and document verification (DigiLocker). It has not solved events. Vigyan Setu is the design argument for solving events.

M08Literature Review

Key finding from crowd dynamics research: At densities exceeding 4 people per square metre, cognitive function drops 60–80%. People stop reading signs, stop making rational routing decisions, and default to following the person in front of them. This explains why signage solutions fail at scale — the problem is neurological, not informational.

End of Discover: 5 Takeaways

01
The failure was systemic, not operational. No amount of better volunteers or bigger signs would have fixed it.
02
India already has the identity rails. Aadhaar, DigiLocker, and DigiYatra prove govt-grade digital identity is deployable at scale.
03
The networking failure is the most expensive one. The entire point of convening 250K people is the connections — and zero infrastructure existed for it.
04
Crowd density changes the design problem. At 4+ people/m², you are designing for impaired cognition, not informed choice.
05
The organizer is also a user. MeitY had no real-time venue state — they were as blind as the delegates.

Phase 03 Develop

"Make many things. Throw most of them out."

M13Crazy 8s

8 home screen directions explored. 2 survived:

  • Action-first: "Here is what happens next." No menu, no options — one verb.
  • Status-first: Dashboard showing your day, your sessions, your network. More information-dense but higher cognitive load.

Action-first won on the principle of "calm under chaos" — when 60K people are in a building, you do not ask them to parse a dashboard.

M14Concept Posters

3 concept contenders tested against design principles:

  • Assistant: Chat-first. "Ask me anything." Failed "proactive over reactive."
  • Dashboard: Grid of tiles. Information-rich. Failed "calm under chaos."
  • Concierge: Anticipates your next move and tells you. Proactive, calm, verified. Won.

M17Information Architecture

3 roles, each with 5 primary nodes:

  • Delegate: Today (concierge), Entry, Map, People, Sessions
  • Organizer: Command Center, Hall Status, Gate Ops, Comms, Analytics
  • Security: Threat Map, VVIP Tracker, Gate Override, Incident Log, Crowd Density
M18Wireflow: Concierge

4 frames: Geofence detect → Today's schedule → Advisory card → Action (route/join/introduce). The user never opens a menu. The app anticipates and presents.

M19Wireflow: Networking

3 frames: Tag yourself ("looking for: ___", "open to: ___") → Ambient match (radar shows nearby relevant people) → One-tap intro (reciprocal consent). Both sides must have matching tags. Distance acts as a natural opt-in filter.

M20Wireflow: Command Center

Organizer dashboard: live venue occupancy heatmap, gate throughput graphs, VVIP movement tracker, session fill rates, and incident log. The digital twin that MeitY did not have during the summit.

M21Design System: "Bharatiya Calm"

Sea-green palette derived from institutional India (government portals, DigiLocker). Tricolor accents used sparingly for status indicators. Roboto for UI, Roboto Mono for data. High-contrast dark theme as default (outdoor readability). Light theme available. Every text pair passes WCAG AA.

M22AI Behavior Spec

6 DO/NEVER pairs governing the AI concierge:

DO
Anticipate the next action based on schedule + location
NEVER
Ask "What would you like to do?"
DO
Re-route silently when disruptions occur
NEVER
Announce a disruption without offering a resolution
DO
Surface networking matches with mutual consent
NEVER
Reveal a user's location to someone they haven't opted into
DO
Degrade gracefully when offline
NEVER
Show a "no connection" error without a fallback action
DO
Log every data access in the consent ledger
NEVER
Access data without explaining why in plain language
DO
Respect a revoked permission instantly
NEVER
Use a dark pattern to discourage permission revocation
M23Accessibility Audit
  • Vision: High-contrast themes, scalable fonts, screen reader compatible
  • Mobility: Ramp-only routing, large touch targets (≥48px), one-handed operation
  • Language: 12 Indian languages + 4 international. Voice navigation toggleable.
  • Digital literacy: Minimal text, icon-led UI, no nested menus
M24Trust & Consent Architecture

5 layers, each auditable:

  • User Layer: Toggle permissions, view audit log, revoke anytime
  • App Layer: Consent ledger, plain-language descriptions, graceful degradation
  • Data Layer: k-anonymity ≥ 50 for aggregated data, no PII in analytics
  • Identity Layer: UIDAI verification, DigiLocker scoped APIs, event-grade tokens
  • Law Layer: DPDP Act 2023 compliance, consent expiry at event end

What I'd Measure

01
Entry-to-first-session time. The real metric is not gate speed — it is how quickly a delegate goes from arriving at the venue to sitting in their first meaningful session.
02
Introduction-to-follow-up rate. Networking intros are vanity metrics unless they convert to follow-up meetings or emails within 48 hours.
03
Consent ledger engagement. If fewer than 20% of users ever open the privacy ledger, the transparency promise is performative.

What I'd Kill

01
Face match at gate. If QR + NFC offline achieves the same throughput, biometric entry adds privacy risk for no UX gain.
02
AI session summaries. If the real summary click rate is below 15%, the feature is cost without value.
03
The radar animation. If user testing shows the radar metaphor confuses rather than clarifies, replace it with a simple list sorted by distance.
§ 05 REFLECTIONS // What I learned, what I'd change

Challenges I Faced

01
No first-party access. I could not interview MeitY organizers or CRPF security. All insights came from secondary sources and public reporting.
02
Speculative identity rails. The UIDAI event-grade API does not exist yet. I designed as though it would, acknowledging the assumption.
03
Indoor positioning uncertainty. BLE + visual SLAM at this venue scale is plausible but unproven. A pilot would validate or force a different approach.
04
Solo execution. A project this broad ideally involves a researcher, an engineer, and a service designer. I wore all four hats, which means depth was traded for breadth in places.
05
The political dimension. Government event infrastructure is politically sensitive. A real engagement would need stakeholder buy-in that this portfolio exercise could not simulate.

Assumptions (Confidence-Rated)

Assumptions I'm making.

A portfolio that hides its assumptions is selling fiction as fact. Here is what I am treating as given — and where a real engagement would start with research instead of certainty.

Confidence: Medium

UIDAI / DigiLocker will issue event-grade APIs

The trust layer assumes government identity rails will expose consented APIs for delegate verification. Aadhaar Authentication exists; event-scoped scopes do not yet.

Confidence: High

Attendees will tolerate proactive guidance

Based on Aarogya Setu and DigiYatra adoption — Indians accept government-issued utility apps when the value is immediate.

Confidence: Low

Indoor positioning will be accurate enough

BLE beacons + visual SLAM at Bharat Mandapam scale is plausible but unproven. A live pilot would either validate this or force a different navigation paradigm.

Confidence: Medium

Organizers will share live telemetry

The B2G command center needs hall occupancy, gate flow, and VVIP movement data. Plausible for a national mission, hard for private organizers.

Confidence: High

The ten reported failure modes are representative

Sourced from public attendee threads, media post-mortems, and exhibitor complaints. A real engagement would triangulate with first-party interviews.

Confidence: Low

A privacy ledger will satisfy the compliance overlap

The consent UX is designed against DPDP Act 2023 principles. International delegates surface GDPR overlaps not modeled in this prototype.

Reflections

01
The best design work is invisible. If Vigyan Setu works, nobody notices it. The absence of chaos is the product.
02
Government-scale design requires government-scale humility. I cannot simulate the political, bureaucratic, and security constraints that would shape a real deployment.
03
Speculative design earns its keep by being honest about what it doesn't know. The assumptions section is as important as the prototype.
04
India's digital public infrastructure is uniquely positioned for this. No other country has UPI, Aadhaar, DigiLocker, and DigiYatra in the same stack. The rails exist; the event OS does not.
05
The Double Diamond is not a template — it is a discipline. Staying in Discover for 2 weeks when the instinct is to start wireframing on Day 2 is the hardest and most valuable part of the process.
