The Personalization Maturity Model

The five stages of enterprise personalization — and an honest way to tell which one you're actually at.

Three years running Personalization at Scale taught me that most orgs think they’re further along than they are. They call rules ‘AI’ and segments ‘one-to-one’.

The model is a plain ladder so a team can locate itself honestly — and see the next rung, instead of skipping straight to the fantasy at the top.

01

The problem

‘Personalization’ means five different things to five people in the same meeting, so the roadmap argues past itself.

You can’t plan the jump to adaptive experiences if you won’t admit you’re still hand-writing segment rules. Most maturity models fail because they flatter. This one is built to be honest.

02

The model

Five stages, each a real capability rather than a slogan. Static (one experience for everyone) → Segmented (a few audiences) → Rules (if-this-then-that, written by hand) → Adaptive (the system learns and adjusts) → Anticipatory (it acts before the user asks).

Each rung has a data-and-trust cost the rung below it has to have already paid. That’s why you can’t skip one.

Five stages, each a real capability with a prerequisite. Higher isn't always the goal.
03

Principles

  1. You can’t skip a rung

    Anticipatory built on shaky data is just confident nonsense at scale.

  2. Each stage has a cost

    Data, plumbing, and earned trust. Pay it before you climb, not after the incident.

  3. Higher isn’t always the goal

    Match the rung to the stakes of the decision. Some experiences should stay simple.

  4. Name your stage out loud

    Honesty about where you are is the entire point of the ladder.

04

How I use it

Use it to locate the team, not to grade it. Agree on today’s rung with data in hand, not with optimism.

Then pick the one prerequisite that unlocks the next rung, and make that the roadmap. One rung at a time beats a leap to ‘anticipatory’ that quietly runs on hand-written rules.
