Framework · FW-03 Human-in-the-loop

The Human Override Matrix

A map from stakes and reversibility to the right amount of human control — decision by decision, not by vibe.

‘Human in the loop’ is a slogan until you decide which humans, in which loop, for which decisions.

Put a person in front of everything and you’ve built a slower version of the old thing. Put a person in front of nothing and you’ll eventually explain to a regulator why not. The matrix is where you make that call one decision at a time, instead of by temperament.

01

The problem

Automation questions get answered by personality — the cautious PM confirms everything, the bold one ships full autonomy — when they should be answered by the shape of the decision.

Two things actually matter: how much is at stake, and whether you can take it back. Almost everything else is noise dressed up as nuance.

02

The model

A 2×2. Stakes on one axis, reversibility on the other. Four zones fall out.

Act (low stakes, reversible): just do it. Notify (reversible, higher stakes): act, but tell them. Confirm (irreversible, lower stakes): ask first. Require human (irreversible, high stakes): don’t move without a person. You place each decision on the grid — not each feature.

Stakes against reversibility. The word that changes everything is 'irreversible'.
03

Principles

  1. Reversible and low-stakes? Just act

    Asking permission for something trivial and undoable is its own kind of rude.

  2. Irreversible changes the rules

    When you can’t undo it, a human signs. No exceptions worth the incident report.

  3. Escalate the decision, not the task

    Route the 5% that’s genuinely hard; automate the 95% that isn’t.

  4. The override has to be reachable

    Control that lives three screens deep in settings isn’t control.

04

How I use it

List the real decisions a feature makes — not the screens, the decisions. That list is usually shorter and scarier than people expect.

Drop each one onto the grid. Anything that lands in ‘Require human’ needs a genuine, usable control at the moment it’s needed — not a disabled button and an apology.
