§ 01 Work transformation stories — challenge to outcome

Selected engagements where the work moved a metric and changed how the org worked. Four signature cases told in detail; three supporting engagements summarised below.

01 AI · Future-State Experience Design Building Dell's AI-Design & Innovation Centre of Excellence Dell Technologies · Dell Digital Design · 2023–2026 +
01 · Challenge

AI arrived faster than design could absorb it. Teams were experimenting in silos with no shared craft, evaluation, or point of view on what “good” looked like for AI experiences.

02 · Complexity

Hundreds of designers, multiple business units, competing tools, and an industry narrative that changed monthly. The risk wasn’t inaction — it was scattered, low-trust adoption.

03 · Strategic call

Treat AI fluency as an organisational capability, not a workshop. Build a CoE that makes the right way the easy way — prompt rituals, evaluation rubrics, and sandbox tooling embedded into how design actually works.

04 · Leadership

Conceptualised and led the CoE end to end — securing executive sponsorship, recruiting practitioners, and earning trust across DDD without formal authority over those teams.

05 · Execution

Shipped a working practice: prompt libraries, CO-STAR and Chain-of-Thought frameworks operationalised for designers, evaluation rubrics, and a sandbox portal — adopted across Dell Digital Design rather than presented as a deck.

06 · Outcome

An established centre of excellence with cross-DDD adoption, raising AI design maturity from ad-hoc experimentation to a repeatable, trusted craft.

07 · Reflection

The lasting work wasn’t a feature — it was capability. Build the system that keeps producing good outcomes after you’ve moved on.

02 GenAI · Conversational Experience Dell.com — a GenAI assistant customers actually trust Dell Technologies · 2022–2026 +
01 · Challenge

Embed generative AI into one of the world’s largest commerce experiences — helping customers navigate complex product decisions — without eroding the trust Dell.com depends on.

02 · Complexity

Hallucination risk, B2B and B2C journeys, 26,000+ Dell Premier stores, recommendation accuracy, and the design of conversation itself — guided flows, media, and a canvas beyond simple chat.

03 · Strategic call

Design for trust before delight. Guided conversational constructs, transparent recommendations, and a canvas experience that moves beyond the chat box to reimagine search and discovery.

04 · Leadership

Led the AI experience design team and aligned engineering, research, and business stakeholders on a personalization-driven recommendation model and an MVP that could ship credibly.

05 · Execution

Built the chat component library and AI design system, onboarding flows, right-panel and canvas experiences, and metric-driven enhancements informed by live usability feedback — taking the B2B chatbot live across 26,000 US Dell Premier stores.

06 · Outcome

An MVP launched across three domains with measurable usability gains, personalized recommendations, and a clear winner in user preference testing — recognised with Dell President & Game Changer Awards.

07 · Reflection

People don’t adopt AI because it’s clever. They adopt it when it’s honest about what it knows and stays useful when it doesn’t.

03 Strategic Systems Thinking Personalization at Scale across Dell.com Dell Technologies · 2022–2026 +
01 · Challenge

Make a vast commerce experience feel personal to every visitor — at the scale of millions of sessions — without fragmenting the brand or overwhelming the customer.

02 · Complexity

Multiple businesses, four concurrent experiences, data and privacy constraints, and the perennial tension between personalization that helps and personalization that unsettles.

03 · Strategic call

Treat personalization as a system, not a set of features — intent estimation, adaptive layouts, and behavioural-science nudges governed by a shared model of customer trust.

04 · Leadership

Led 33 designers across five time zones running four experiences concurrently, holding a coherent vision while empowering teams to own their domains.

05 · Execution

Operationalised prompt libraries, intent estimation, multi-modal adaptive layouts, and a measurement discipline that tied design decisions to KPI movement rather than opinion.

06 · Outcome

A personalization practice grounded in behavioural science and trust, recognised internally and adopted as a model for how Dell.com adapts to its customers.

07 · Reflection

Scale is a design material. The discipline is keeping the experience human while the system underneath grows more intelligent.

04 Platform · Product Transformation TAAM — modernising Boeing’s airspace simulation cockpit Boeing · Total Airspace & Airport Modeller · 2016–18 +
01 · Challenge

TAAM — Boeing’s fast-time simulation platform for hub airports, air navigation service providers, and aerodrome consultancies — carried decades of capability behind an interface that experts tolerated rather than trusted.

02 · Complexity

A small, expert global user base; immense domain depth; and the high stakes of aviation planning, where a confusing control isn’t an inconvenience — it’s a costly modelling error.

03 · Strategic call

Respect the expertise, redesign the friction. Anchor the work in real user mental models through a structured discovery before touching a pixel.

04 · Leadership

Ran a day-long design-thinking workshop with stakeholders — Crazy-8s, 5-Whys — to build shared ownership of the problem before proposing a single solution.

05 · Execution

Moved from lo-fi wireframes to high-fidelity prototypes across day, dawn, and dusk modes, validated through restricted usability testing with the TAAM User Group using Think-Aloud protocols and SUS scoring.

06 · Outcome

System usability rose from an SUS of 71 to 84, with a 47% reduction in time-to-first-action — measurable proof that elegance and expert software belong together.

07 · Reflection

Enterprise users don’t want their tools dumbed down. They want the complexity organised, so their expertise has room to work.

§ 03 About the leadership story, told straight

I’m a Principal Product Designer with 20 years at the seam between executives, engineers, and end users — from early printer firmware UIs at H-P R&D, to operations cockpits at Boeing, to GenAI personalization at Dell.com.

Most recently, across nearly eight years at Dell (2018–2026), I led the Personalization at Scale practice for the .com — designing the system where GenAI, behavioural science, analytics, and millions of customers meet.

I believe the next decade of design leadership is about designing with agents in the room as colleagues, not tools — and about building the operating systems (rituals, rubrics, evidence) that let an org stay coherent while it experiments at speed.

“Grow the core, while adding some more.”

§ 03a How I Think six lenses I bring to a design org
01 · Strategy

Systems thinking

The product is a node. The org is the graph. Most product problems are coordination problems wearing UX clothes — fix the coordination, the UX follows.

02 · Alignment

Stakeholder negotiation

Executives, eng SMEs, PMs, sales, customers — I learned at Dell how to hold five truths at once and ship something all five can defend in their own language.

03 · Influence

Design without authority

Most of the leverage I’ve had came before I had a title for it. I run rituals and write memos that change minds long before they change reporting lines.

04 · Craft

Practice as a system

3-6-9 month deliverable plans. Coaching loops. Mentorship rotations. Practice is engineered, not assumed — that’s how 33 designers across 5 time zones stay coherent.

05 · Ambiguity

Enterprise comfort

Re-orgs, dotted lines, missing data, contested OKRs. Most of the work happens before the brief is written. I’m comfortable in the fog and good at drawing the map.

06 · AI

Agents as colleagues

Prompt libraries, intent estimation, evaluation rubrics, sandbox tooling. I treat AI not as a feature to be designed but as a teammate to be onboarded.

§ 03b How I Lead quiet confidence, compounding trust
  1. i.

    I mentor designers into leaders

    Coaching individuals from execution to judgement — the difference between someone who can do the work and someone who can decide what work matters.

  2. ii.

    I influence stakeholders, not just deliverables

    Bringing engineering, product, and the C-suite to a shared experience vision — and to the tradeoffs it demands.

  3. iii.

    I lead cross-functionally

    Thirty-three designers across five time zones, four concurrent experiences — alignment held through clarity, not control.

  4. iv.

    I build for design maturity

    Systems, rituals, and centres of excellence that raise the whole organisation’s craft, long after any single project ends.

  5. v.
