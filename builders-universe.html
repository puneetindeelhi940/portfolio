Galaxy 02 · The efficiency thesis

The next AI revolution is smaller bills, not bigger models.

Everyone is chasing capability. I argue the real advantage is efficiency — People, Planet, Profit. The teams that win won't have the biggest model; they'll have the one that's cheapest to run at quality. Every additional token has a cost, and measuring it is a competitive edge.
The point of view

Triage, don't over-serve

Not every request needs your most expensive model. Classify, score, and route — hospital triage for AI workloads. ~25× cheaper at scale, with only the hard cases escalated.

Efficiency is a first-class metric

Most teams ship agents they can't measure. Agent efficiency — cost, steps, and success per task — belongs on the dashboard next to accuracy.

The invisible AI tax

AI is quietly making everything more expensive. Naming and measuring that cost is the first step to controlling it.

Galaxy 03 · The trust stand

Most AI is clever. Almost none is trusted.

AI's biggest enterprise failure isn't intelligence — it's trust. As interfaces get more persuasive, the line between an ethical nudge and a dark pattern gets thinner. I design for trust before delight, and I build the tools that make manipulation visible.
The point of view

Trust before delight

People don't adopt AI because it's clever. They adopt it when it's honest about what it knows — and stays useful when it doesn't.

Make dark patterns visible

You can't fix what you can't see. AI-driven audits score interfaces for trust erosion and distinguish ethical nudges from manipulative design.

Guardrails for autonomy

Agentic systems need principles, patterns, and human-in-the-loop gates — trust engineered in, not bolted on.
