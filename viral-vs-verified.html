Why this exists

In the age of generative AI, a convincing fake is cheaper to produce than a verified fact is to report. Virality rewards outrage and novelty; verification rewards patience and sourcing. The result is a dark pattern hiding in plain sight — the most shareable version of an event routinely out-travels the true one, and people keep trusting the feed that feeds them.

The left column is ranked by reach and falsehood; the right by trust. Every left-hand item is a real, documented claim that a professional fact-checker rated false or misleading — nothing here is invented. Click any source to read the debunk or the original report.

Left sources — Lead Stories, Snopes, FactCheck.org, Check Your Fact, Science Feedback, Full Fact, Alt News, PolitiFact, AFP.  ·  Right sources — Reuters, BBC News, NPR, PBS NewsHour, AP, The Guardian, Deutsche Welle. Feeds are fetched server-side on an 8-hour schedule; a source is skipped whenever it is temporarily unreachable.
