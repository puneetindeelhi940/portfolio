§ 01 WHAT IS A DARK PATTERN? // the theory, in three parts

A dark pattern (or “deceptive pattern”) is an interface deliberately crafted to trick users into doing things they didn’t mean to — buying more, sharing more, staying subscribed, clicking “agree.” The term was coined by UX researcher Harry Brignull in 2010. These are not accidents or bad usability; they are persuasion weaponised against the user’s own interest. Regulators now treat many of them as unlawful.

01 · THE HOOK

They exploit how brains take shortcuts

Loss aversion, scarcity, social proof, default bias, the sunk-cost fallacy. Dark patterns don’t defeat your reasoning — they slip past it, borrowing the mental shortcuts you use to move quickly.

02 · THE ASYMMETRY

Easy to fall for, hard to reverse

The manipulative path is one giant button; the honest path is a grey link three menus deep. The design is intentionally lopsided so the outcome the business wants is the path of least resistance.

03 · THE HARM

Money, data, attention, autonomy

The cost lands on the user: unexpected charges, over-shared data, wasted time, eroded trust, and decisions that don’t reflect what they actually wanted. At scale, it’s a market failure — which is why it’s regulated.

§ 04 WHY AI CHANGES THE GAME // manipulation that personalises, adapts, and scales itself
OPEN THE AI SIMULATOR →
PERSONALISED

It targets you

Classic dark patterns are one-size-fits-all. AI tailors the manipulation to your data — your hesitation, your history, your emotional state — finding the exact lever that moves you specifically.

ADAPTIVE

It learns what works

An AI system A/B tests you in real time. If guilt didn’t keep you subscribed, it tries urgency, then flattery, then a discount — optimising relentlessly toward the outcome the business wants.

CONVERSATIONAL

It feels like a person

A chatbot that sounds warm, confident and human can extract data, upsell, and discourage cancellation while feeling like help. Anthropomorphism is the delivery mechanism; sycophancy and false confidence are the payload.

The AI simulator lets you interact with reconstructions of these: a chatbot that agrees with everything to keep you engaged, a “retention specialist” that emotionally guilt-trips you out of cancelling, synthetic scarcity generated on the fly, and a conversation quietly engineered to extract more of your data than you intended to give.
