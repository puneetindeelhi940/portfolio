§ 02 · THE CHALLENGE

What problem were we solving?

// the starting point

As Dell scaled digital support and product guidance experiences, customers encountered multiple disconnected virtual assistants — each tied to a specific domain or entry point. This created friction for both users and the business.

BUSINESS PROBLEM
  • Missed conversion opportunities from users who don’t receive timely, relevant product guidance
  • Increased contact center load due to easily answerable queries escalating unnecessarily
  • Inconsistent branding and experience across touchpoints reduces user trust
USER PROBLEM
  • Fragmented experiences lead to confusion — users don’t know which assistant can help with what
  • Users repeat themselves and feel frustrated when switching channels or getting routed without context
  • Low confidence in self-service options due to lack of clarity or follow-through
Our goal: create one unified, scalable AI assistant to guide users across any entry point, reduce friction, and build consistency, trust, and clarity.

The Double Diamond approach

Our process followed the Double Diamond — diverging to explore broadly, then converging to act decisively. Each phase built on the last, from early research all the way through to global rollout.

§ 03 · DISCOVER

Understanding the landscape.

// AI patterns, competitive signals, current state

Before we could design for AI, we needed to truly understand it. The team grounded itself in real-world AI design principles, competitive signals, and the existing product experience.

“If we’re designing for AI, we need to truly understand it.”

AI Patterns & Design Principles

For the MVP, the team studied real-world AI design principles — including the Microsoft HAX Toolkit — to understand how the most successful systems create user trust, set realistic expectations, and handle uncertainty with care. Guidelines covering Goal Clarity, Failure Transparency, and Experience Grounding helped us look beyond functionality and into the behaviour and communication of AI systems.

Key takeaways from the research:

  • Clearly state what the assistant can and can’t do, up front and clearly
  • When the system explains itself in the moment, users know what to expect and trust grows
  • Users want to understand why the AI is making a recommendation — not just what it recommends
INITIALLY
Make clear what the system can do · Make clear how well it can do it · Convey consequences of user actions
DURING INTERACTION
Time services based on context · Show contextually relevant information · Make clear why the system did what it did
WHEN WRONG
Support efficient correction · Scope services when in doubt · Mitigate social biases · Match relevant social norms
OVER TIME
Remember recent interactions · Learn from user behaviour · Update and adapt cautiously · Notify users about changes

Evaluating the Current State

To ground the work, the team evaluated the current experience already in production — a lightweight Proof-of-Concept (PoC) live on the Unified Product Display (UPD) pages. The team assessed the existing flow and identified areas for improvement in both the user interface and in how effectively the assistant responded to user needs.

Three friction points were identified:

  • Lack of Clarity — users didn’t understand what the assistant could do
  • Poor Engagement — interaction rates were low, starting at just 4.4%
  • Missed Conversion Opportunities — the assistant wasn’t driving purchasing decisions

§ 04 · DEFINE

Framing the opportunity.

// scope, outcomes, philosophy

Research insights converged into a clear problem framing. The team aligned on scope, objectives, and expected outcomes — creating a shared north star for everything that followed.

MVP Scope & Objective

  • Create a seamless and unified customer experience to minimise contacts arising from easily answerable quick questions and from misrouted inquiries due to mixed intents across various domains
  • Monitor the experience to ensure a balance between the need to drive self-service and a premium customer experience
  • Enable an experience that can be scaled to other customer types (e.g. ISG) and journeys (e.g. Premier)

Expected Outcomes

The team framed success from three perspectives:

AS A CUSTOMER

I access the right tool, answer, or resource regardless of my entry point or current location at any online Dell platform.

AS AN AGENT

I get routed the appropriate customer need and have context as to what they experienced online to best assist them.

AS A SYSTEM

I can spot where customers are having trouble and figure out where to improve the experience.

Design Philosophy

With the problem and scope defined, one core belief shaped everything that came next: the team was committed to being grounded in research and learning this evolution of technology — not just on how to build a GenAI-powered conversational experience, but how to build one that is intuitive and that users can trust.

§ 05 · DEVELOP

Designing, iterating & testing.

// six stages, end-to-end

With a clear brief in hand, the team began designing — starting with lightweight UI improvements, then evolving into a fully branded system, tested extensively with real users.

Stage 1 — Lightweight UI improvements

The initial focus was on lightweight UI improvements aimed at simplifying the user journey, enhancing readability, and improving response clarity — essential elements that build user trust and encourage engagement with AI.

Chat Initiation Screen improvements:

  • Improved Minimize Icon — updated the minimize icon design to make it more intuitive
  • Clear Chat Capabilities — made the assistant’s purpose more prominent so users instantly understand what the AI can help with
  • Transparency Placement — moved the transparency disclaimer higher for better visibility before users begin chatting
  • Refined Chat Guidelines Box — updated the appearance to feel more integrated and readable, rather than a popup overlay
  • Clickable Prompt Buttons — replaced text links with buttons; users found them easier to notice and interact with during testing
  • Character Counter Enhancement — added a live character count to help users track input as they type

End Chat & Survey Screen improvements:

  • Removed question mark from header — to make the heading clearer and more direct
  • Simplified the survey — eliminated the binary “Yes/No” question to reduce friction
  • New primary action: “Begin Survey” — prominent button to let users know there is a way to share feedback
  • Feedback textbox shown upfront — the optional feedback field is now visible before the rating is selected
  • Changed experience rating to CSAT — reframed the question using a 1 to 5 scale from Poor to Excellent

Validating the Design Hypothesis

Design changes made a measurable impact — engagement rose from 4.4% to 80%. The improvements confirmed that better readability, simpler flows, and clearer responses drive meaningful user engagement.

Stage 2 — Branding the AI experience

Even after early improvements, the experience didn’t visually reflect intelligence, trust, or personality. This opened the door for a deeper exploration into branding — a strategic opportunity to go beyond aesthetics and signal clarity, trust, and innovation through how the assistant looks.

STEP 01

The Challenge

The assistant didn’t feel “intelligent” or “trustworthy” at a glance — a critical gap, even after early improvements. Branding wasn’t in the original scope, but the team saw a chance to elevate user perception.

STEP 02

Exploration

The team began testing palettes, gradients, and treatments — shaping how the AI experience feels while aligning with tone and voice. All visual explorations stayed grounded in the Dell Design System (DDS), while pushing toward a more expressive and futuristic AI personality.

STEP 03

The Question

One question shaped the visual strategy: do we stay with Dell’s classic blues, or explore new visual territory that signals AI? The team knew branding influences how users perceive intelligence, trust, and innovation.

90%

The purple gradient won.

After countless explorations, critiques, and rounds of feedback, the purple AI gradient emerged as the clear winner in user testing. Users described it as “fresh and modern,” “feels like AI without even reading anything,” “visually stands out on Dell.com,” and “feels smarter.” The branding wasn’t just a preference — it was clarity: users instantly recognised the gradient as something “AI-powered,” making the assistant feel distinct yet connected to the Dell ecosystem.

Stage 3 — Designing the AI icon

The next challenge was choosing the right icon — something that signals AI clearly and confidently. Initial options leaned on existing DDS assets or reused icons from the PoC, but they fell flat in testing.

Stage 4 — Naming the chatbot

The team explored potential names through two separate studies to understand what resonates most with users globally and internally.

USER STUDY · BRANDING TEAM

Across 4 regions. “Dell Virtual Assistant” was the top choice in most regions, scoring the highest preference across China, the US, Germany, and Japan. It was valued for building clarity and trust.

USER STUDY · AI DESIGN TEAM

“Dell AI” emerged as the top choice for modern and tech-forward appeal — scoring highest both when prompted and when users were asked unprompted what they’d call a Dell chatbot.

“Dell Virtual Assistant” is clear and easy to understand globally, while “Dell AI” feels sleek, modern, and comes up naturally in user responses. Both insights were kept in mind as plans for the chatbot’s future identity took shape.

§ 06 · DELIVER

Launching, measuring & scaling.

// MVP, production metrics, enhancements, expansion

The Unified AI Assistant launched in Q3 FY25. After tracking production metrics, the team iterated with post-MVP enhancements and expanded the assistant across the Dell.com ecosystem.

MVP Launch — Q3 FY25

The Unified AI Assistant launched across three key domains:

Shop & Learn

The Chat Initiation screen sets clear expectations upfront — highlighting what the assistant can help with, dynamically tailored to each page. It includes Dell’s transparency statement and pre-suggested prompts to guide users.

My Account

Response screen includes feedback and accessibility tools. Users can rate responses with thumbs up or down to improve future answers, and use the speak-aloud feature for enhanced accessibility and ease of use.

Support

The end chat screen sets clear expectations and encourages feedback. Users are informed upfront that the chat won’t be saved or resumed. Before exiting, they’re invited to take a quick survey — helping gather valuable feedback to improve the experience.

Post-MVP Enhancements

After reviewing production metrics, clear opportunities emerged to improve the experience. Some gaps called for deeper user feedback, while others revealed the need for better conversation design. This led to a mix of metric-driven and design-led enhancements.

METRIC-DRIVEN · 01

Thumbs Up / Down Feedback Redesign

In the MVP, thumbs up/down responses were simply recorded as binary feedback. When 55% of users marked responses as not helpful, the team needed more context. The feedback flow was redesigned so users can now select a specific reason (e.g., irrelevant answer, inaccurate information, other) and optionally leave comments. This gives the team actionable insight and helps tune the model for better output quality.

Breakdown of thumbs-down feedback categories: Irrelevant answer · Inaccurate information · Other — providing targeted insight to prioritise fixes and refine AI responses in real-world use.

METRIC-DRIVEN · 02

End Chat Survey — Embedded Flow

The MVP experience required users to click “Begin Survey” after ending the chat. This additional step significantly reduced participation. To improve feedback volume, the end-chat experience was reimagined — the survey is now embedded directly into the flow, reducing friction and encouraging more users to share their experience.

DESIGN-LED · 01

Post-Response Prompts

The team identified the need for follow-up prompts during contextual research, where it was noticed that assistants offering relevant next steps increased depth of engagement. This design enhancement adds contextual follow-up suggestions after each response — placing users towards meaningful next steps, increasing overall conversation engagement. In user testing, more than 85% of participants tagged this feature as very useful and said it helped them do more of what they came to do next.

Note: This feature is currently in the backlog and planned for rollout soon.

DESIGN-LED · 02

AI-Powered Product Recommendations

Product recommendations were included in the design roadmap early on, knowing the VA would evolve into a shopping assistant. The team studied how industry-leading assistants structured their recommendations and analysed how LLMs typically respond to such prompts. Through multiple iterations and A/B testing, the team learned that users don’t just want product suggestions — they want to understand why something is being recommended.

Three rounds of usability testing were conducted, experimenting with different layouts, content depth, and tone. A standout feature was the “Why Choose It” section — which clearly explained the reasoning behind each suggestion. In the final design:

  • 96% of users said the “Why Choose It” section made decision-making easier and helped them feel more confident about choosing the right product
  • Users preferred scannable, visually organised responses over dense text lists
  • The final design balanced product imagery, key specs, price, and contextual reasoning

Note: This feature is currently in the backlog and planned for rollout soon.

Beyond the chatbot — new modalities

After the MVP launched, a clear pattern emerged: users didn’t want the assistant to disappear when they moved between pages. This became a key turning point — the assistant needed to be a companion. The team began exploring leading brands tackling similar challenges and reimagined new interaction models that could feel fluid, flexible, and always available.

Side Panel

A full-height side panel assistant that keeps support present and helpful without disrupting the flow of the page. Offers contextual recommendations through rich product cards, more conversational space, and summarised guidance to speed up decision-making. Tested with users from IT, Finance, and Operations.

90% reported high satisfaction 90% preferred right-side placement

Full-Screen Experience

An immersive chat mode designed for deeper collaboration, discovery, and guided workflows. Built for higher-effort use cases like research, decision-making, or long-form guidance. Same conversation, same assistant — but now optimised for focus and ease of thinking.

70% wanted richer visuals integrated into chat

Canvas Experience

A visual workspace built into the assistant — letting users open and compare multiple products side by side, view rich content like PDFs, videos, and interactive cards, and stay in context while exploring, without needing to switch tabs or lose their conversation.

Use cases: 3D Viewer · GenAI Summariser · PDF Viewer · Media Links · Success Stories

Guided Conversations

As user journeys became more complex, so did the need for structure and clarity. What began as open-ended chat interfaces evolved into guided conversations — purposeful, goal-driven flows designed to reduce decision fatigue, surface relevant options, and offer just the right amount of support at every step.

Guided conversations enable:

  • Intent-Based Navigation — users get help finding the right path without needing to know exactly what they’re looking for
  • Personalised Recommendations — the assistant adapts to user type (e.g., business, IT, creative) and offers tailored solutions
  • Natural Interaction — users engage through intuitive dialogue, like asking a trusted advisor
  • Business Outcomes — designed to be more accurate upstream, reduce downstream returns, and decrease post-purchase support

After early research and iterative conversation design testing, a repeatable framework was identified — a guided conversational loop that starts by uncovering the user’s needs, navigates them through targeted AI-related topics, and progressively builds toward actionable recommendations.

Pan-Dell Expansion

A natural progression in the journey was expanding the assistant across key pages — meeting users where they are with contextual support, tailored guidance, and seamless entry points throughout the shopping and discovery flow.

LIVE

Premier Connect

A next-generation self-service experience for commercial customers, now live across more than 26,000 US Premier Stores. Blends conversational AI with human expertise, unifying support across CSG, ISG, and Account domains. Customer research revealed the need for a hybrid experience — quick AI-driven answers for simple queries and smooth handoffs to Sales for complex needs.

LIVE

Alienware

Following the success of UAI on Dell.com, the team extended the assistant to Alienware — launched in Q1 FY26. Reused foundational logic and interaction patterns from the UAI component library. The UI was customised to reflect Alienware’s visual identity (teal and purple palette), while maintaining trust, accessibility, and performance standards consistent with the core UAI experience.

PLANNED

GenAI-Powered Search

Many users begin their journey with vague or ambiguous search terms, quickly becoming overwhelmed by options — leading to decision fatigue early in the experience. The team began using AI to better understand user intent, deliver more relevant results, and guide users toward confident decisions, reducing overwhelm and making the experience more intuitive.
